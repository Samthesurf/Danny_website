About

Why we exist, and how we got here.

DACTION Initiative is a youth-led environmental nonprofit based in Lekki, Lagos. We design and deliver hands-on programs across Nigeria, built and run by young people, for the communities and ecosystems they affect.

Mission

Turn environmental awareness into action people can see.

We design and deliver youth-led programs, from beach cleanups and reforestation to clean energy education and soil restoration, that produce real, visible outcomes for the communities and ecosystems we work in.

Vision

Young Africans leading the continent's environmental future.

We're working toward a future where young people across Africa combine scientific training, strong community partnerships, and the confidence to execute, so they lead the continent's response to environmental and energy challenges rather than simply taking part in it.

Our story

It didn't start with a plan. It started with a question.

DACTION Initiative began in June 2021, as more of a question than a plan. Daniel Olajuyigbe, then an undergraduate engineering student at Afe Babalola University, kept noticing the same gap wherever he looked: young Nigerians cared deeply about the environment around them, but almost none of the programs meant to engage them were actually designed by young people. Most were run by adults, for children, with little room for youth to lead.

He started DACTION to close that gap: an organization that young people would run themselves, from choosing what to work on, to raising the funding, to answering honestly for what worked and what didn't.

The first real test of that idea came a year later. In 2022, DACTION won its first international grant and partnered with RESWAYE to bring ocean conservation education and recycling infrastructure into five secondary schools in Akure. It wasn't the mission the organization was built around so much as proof that a small, youth-led team could plan a real campaign and see it through.

From there, the work kept moving toward whatever gap felt most urgent that year. Coastal cleanups and reforestation in Lekki followed in 2024. A hybrid energy dialogue on hydrogen's place in Nigeria's future came in 2025, pulling in an NNPC executive and researchers from three countries. Now, DACTION is preparing a soil and food-systems summit at a Nigerian university, and has taken on its first institutional funding commitment, backing a new Model United Nations association at Daniel's alma mater.

What's stayed constant through every shift is the method: young people design the campaign, a small team executes it directly, and the organization reports back plainly on what actually happened, not just what was hoped for.

DACTION today is still a volunteer-led organization, run alongside its founder's engineering studies and career, built by people who believe environmental leadership shouldn't have to wait for permission.

Beach cleanups across five sites in Lekki and Ajah, a 20-tree reforestation planting at a public secondary school, and a public artivism event blending pottery, painting, and climate education.

DACTION's first campaign: ocean conservation education, donated recycling bins, and a student artivism competition delivered across five secondary schools in Akure, Ondo State.

Why we work across so many fronts

The problems aren't separate, so we don't treat them that way.

Since 2021, DACTION's work has touched ocean conservation, coastal cleanups, reforestation, renewable energy education, and soil health. That range isn't a lack of focus. A community without recycling infrastructure is often the same community facing coastal erosion, energy poverty, and food insecurity, and treating those as one connected problem lets a small, youth-led team respond to whatever gap is most urgent right now, instead of being boxed into a single issue while everything around it goes unaddressed.

It's also why DACTION stays open to new partnerships, new geographies, and new forms of environmental storytelling, from beach cleanups to hydrogen energy dialogues to youth artivism competitions. We follow the need and the opportunity for real impact wherever they lead, while holding onto the same throughline in everything we do: youth leadership, direct community partnership, and honest reporting on what actually happened.

Looking ahead, DACTION is actively growing on several fronts at once: deeper ocean and coastal conservation work, expanded renewable energy advocacy, and more youth artivism and storytelling projects that put creative work in service of environmental awareness. We're building on partnerships like the Captain Planet Foundation while staying open to new collaborations that share our commitment to youth-led, evidence-based environmental action.

If your organization is working on ocean conservation, clean energy, climate storytelling, or youth leadership development, and you think there's common ground with what we do, we'd like to hear from you.
